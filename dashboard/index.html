The managed policy — what changes

  • One declared tolerance per material, set from consumption volatility at a 95% service level ( on average) — instead of three stacked guesses ( in total).
  • A deal gate: take extra quantity for a supplier offer only if the discount beats the cost of the cash it ties up, capped at 10% of the need.
  • One transparent ledger: every PO records need → each layer → purchase (fact_replenishment_chain), so each tolerance has an owner and a number, and the gap is visible before the money is spent.

Layer tolerances, deal odds and the 55%/yr cost of cash are documented simulation assumptions (analytics/bullwhip.py) — replace them with your ERP's PO history to get the real numbers.

Average CCC, last 12 weeks of data
Raw materials (DIO) barely move the number — short shelf life keeps finished-goods stock near zero, so inventory isn't where the cash is tied up.

The real driver is the gap between how slowly retail/wholesale customers pay (DSO) and how quickly perishable-ingredient suppliers must be paid (DPO) — dairy suppliers run near cash-on-delivery, while large retail chains routinely stretch 50–90 days.

This is computed from simulated receivables and payables ledgers (analytics/working_capital.py), not asserted — the same inventory and purchasing decisions the rest of this engine plans around are what move this number.
