Forecast vs. planned output, by week

Bars: units. Line: fill rate. The two shaded weeks are the ones where demand outruns what the plant can actually make.

Every ordinary week, the plan meets 100% of forecast. The two weeks before Nowruz are different: forecast jumps to 55,610 and 54,061 units while the plant's real capacity holds — fill rate drops to 88.5% and 91.0%, worth ~690M IRR in unmet demand across the fortnight.

Where the shortfall actually happens

All five lines forecast fine except one: the Traditional Kolucheh Line. Its capacity (tuned to a realistic hand-folded-pastry rate) simply can't clear both peak weeks at once.

Which SKUs absorb it

The LP's call: fully serve the higher-margin Nowruz specialty items first. Cardamom and Plain Butter Kolucheh — the two lowest-priced varieties on the line — absorb the entire shortfall instead.

Average CCC, last 12 weeks of data
Raw materials (DIO) barely move the number — short shelf life keeps finished-goods stock near zero, so inventory isn't where the cash is tied up.

The real driver is the gap between how slowly retail/wholesale customers pay (DSO) and how quickly perishable-ingredient suppliers must be paid (DPO) — dairy suppliers run near cash-on-delivery, while large retail chains routinely stretch 50–90 days.

This is computed from simulated receivables and payables ledgers (analytics/working_capital.py), not asserted — the same inventory and purchasing decisions the rest of this engine plans around are what move this number.
